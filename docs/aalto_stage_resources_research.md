# Aalto University resources for a 60–90 second *White Hole* sensory stage slice

**Research date:** 3 September 2026  
**Scope:** official/first-party Aalto University, Aalto Studios, Aalto Design Factory, AYY and named association sources only.  
**Prototype constraint:** colour, directional light, projection and sound are not embellishments; they are part of the minimum dramatic mechanism. The recommended experiment is therefore a complete sensory slice, not a technology-free acting test.

## Executive finding

Aalto already has nearly the whole physical stack needed for the first test: bookable AV equipment, RGB/bi-colour fixtures, DMX interfaces, projectors/screens, portable loudspeakers, microphones, VR headsets, Vive trackers, a darkenable 110 m² studio with installed lighting and projection, a 360° projection room with 9.1 audio, an immersive-audio studio, motion-capture infrastructure, and set/costume workshops. The constraint is **permission and coordination**, not hardware scarcity.

The shortest credible route is:

1. create an Aalto Takeout project and make a small portable equipment request;
2. request one supervised 2-hour test in **Marsio Experimental Studio 1** while keeping **Design Factory Stage** as the lower-friction fallback;
3. recruit one lighting/sound technician from **Teekkarispeksi** and one interactive-media collaborator through **DADA or DOT**;
4. run one cue sequence: cold darkness → warm directional window light and green-table projection → sound approaches → image/light fractures → blackout/release;
5. test VR and precise motion capture only after this shared-stage sequence works.

Do not plan around the old VR Hub or GVRIPA minor: the VR Hub closed in 2024, and the GVRIPA minor was formally discontinued in 2021. The successor XR facility is the Marsio Extended Reality Studio. ([VR Hub](https://studios.aalto.fi/fi/vr-hub/), [GVRIPA status](https://www.aalto.fi/en/minors/games-and-virtual-reality-in-performing-arts-gvripa))

## Access labels used below

- **Confirmed student-accessible:** the official source explicitly includes Aalto students, though a login, reservation, induction or item permit may still be required.
- **Conditional:** a teacher, responsible staff member, project status, training, production budget or research permission is required.
- **Uncertain:** the facility exists, but the public source does not confirm that an independent student project can book it. Ask before designing around it.

## Equipment and sensory-production resources

### 1. Light, colour, projection and playback — confirmed via Takeout

The public Takeout inventory currently shows, among other items:

- RGB lighting: a **10-unit Multiform Multispot RGB LED kit** and individual RGB panels;
- directional/film lighting: Desisti 300 W and 650 W Fresnels, Nanlite FS-200 daylight spot, DMG Lumière and Sachtler lighting kits;
- control: Enttec DMX USB Pro interfaces, an Enttec Ethernet ODE and 5-pin DMX cables;
- projection/display: portable screens, media players and a `Projector & TV` inventory category;
- sound: portable PA, multiple Genelec kits, wireless/lavalier/shotgun microphones, Zoom and other recorders;
- spatial capture: Sennheiser AMBEO VR microphone, DPA binaural headset, Zoom H3-VR and Zylia 6DoF VR/AR set.

This establishes that the equipment exists in the lending system, **not** that every item is automatically permitted or available on the desired day. Availability and user-group restrictions must be checked after Aalto login. The official service says some items are restricted by user group or require training/permit. ([live inventory](https://takeout.aalto.fi/), [access policy](https://www.aalto.fi/en/services/studios-takeout))

**Minimum request list for the first slice:** one bright projector + screen/surface; one focusable directional fixture or LED Fresnel; two RGB fixtures or one RGB kit; stands/safety accessories; one laptop-to-DMX interface; two active speakers or portable PA; audio interface/recorder; one camera/tripod to document the run. Ask Takeout staff to substitute safe equivalents rather than naming professional cinema equipment that your project may not be permitted to borrow.

### 2. Haze: available but do not assume permission

Takeout lists MDG Atmosphere APS hazers and a fog generator, but the MDG page places the unit under a professional-cinema permit and says access requires expertise and particular project status. No reviewed public page grants permission to operate haze in Experimental Studio 1, Design Factory Stage or another proposed room. ([hazer resource](https://takeout.aalto.fi/?i=613923))

Therefore haze is **conditional and non-critical**. Ask the venue in writing about fire detection isolation, approved fluid, ventilation, technician operation and reset costs. If any answer is unclear, achieve visible beams with angle, contrast and particulate-free scenic surfaces instead.

## A concrete 2–4 week resource plan

### Week 1 — secure a reversible production path

1. Log in to [Aalto Takeout](https://takeout.aalto.fi/), create project **White Hole — 90-second interactive sensory stage test**, and inspect actual availability for two candidate weeks.
2. Email `studios-takeout@aalto.fi` with: one-sentence artistic question; exact 90-second cue; dates; one actor + two operators; portable equipment list; statement that there is no haze unless venue-approved; request for item substitutions and required permits.
3. Email Rolando, `studios@aalto.fi`, and your teacher/supervisor requesting **one supervised two-hour Experimental Studio 1 slot**, not a full production booking. Attach a floor sketch, cue list, equipment list and safety note.
4. In parallel, submit the Design Factory Stage booking form and ask `spaces-df@aalto.fi` whether blackout, freestanding fixtures, taped cable runs and amplified playback are allowed.
5. Send three collaborator messages: Teekkarispeksi (lighting/sound/stage manager), DADA/DOT (cue programmer), and one dance/theatre group (performer). Ask for one rehearsal, not an open-ended project commitment.

### Week 2 — previsualise and dry-wire

In a normal student-bookable room or Design Factory slot:

- lock a five-cue sequence and exact timings;
- run visuals from the existing Three.js prototype or a rendered fallback video;
- programme one deterministic lighting transition via DMX or manual operator cue;
- make a stereo sound file with clear approach/withdraw movement;
- rehearse with a human `GO/HOLD/BLACKOUT` operator;
- record system latency and reset time.

The fallback video and manual cue are mandatory: the sensory experience should survive WebSocket, browser or tracking failure.

### Week 3 — technical rehearsal in the best approved room

Run 6–10 repetitions in Experimental Studio 1 or the confirmed fallback. Capture:

- actor movement and cue timestamps;
- whether green/table colour remains legible against projection;
- whether the directional “window” light visibly motivates the actor’s approach;
- whether sound location/level is perceptible without overwhelming the voice;
- whether the image/light fracture reads as a changed memory state;
- reset time and any unsafe cable/vision conditions.

Do **not** add VR, mocap or generative AI during this session unless the room, people and cue sequence are already stable.

### Week 4 — one comparison, not a feature pile

Choose exactly one:

- **shared immersion:** repeat the slice in Visualisation Studio;
- **private memory:** a single Quest 3 version;
- **embodied trigger:** one Vive Tracker or camera trigger replacing the operator’s `GO`.

Keep the story, duration, audio and basic colour transition constant. Compare observer comprehension, actor agency, failure rate and setup/reset time.

## Definition of done for the first resource-backed test

The test is complete when:

- one actor performs a 60–90 second arc with a real green object;
- directional light, colour, projection and sound each carry a named dramatic function;
- at least one actor action or operator-confirmed action changes the sensory state;
- the run can be repeated three times with the same cue order;
- it has a manual fallback and a blackout/stop control;
- three observers can say what changed in the character’s relationship to the memory, not merely list the technologies;
- setup plus reset fits the booked slot and all venue safety rules.

## Resource gaps that Aalto hardware does not solve

1. **A named responsible teacher/producer.** This is the key that unlocks staff-managed studios and makes the project legible as study/artistic research.
2. **A stage manager/cue caller.** Sensor automation is not a substitute for safe repeatable rehearsal.
3. **Rights and consent.** Confirm rights for voice/music/images and consent for performer/video/movement recording.
4. **A lighting concept translated into fixtures.** “Warm memory” is not yet a focus, angle, colour, intensity and transition time.
5. **A projection surface test.** A real green table, actor skin and projected image may fight for contrast; test before fabricating scenery.
6. **A venue-approved safety plan.** Cable ramps/tape, sightlines, emergency stop, rigging authority, sound limits and haze/fire-alarm rules are local decisions.
7. **A stable operator stack.** TouchDesigner/Unity/Three.js, DMX, playback and tracking need one accountable integration owner.

## Questions to ask locally before committing

Send these as a compact checklist, not as separate exploratory emails:

1. Can an independent Aalto student artistic-research project receive a temporary permit for Experimental Studio 1? Must a teacher be present or only sponsor the booking?
2. What is the earliest two-hour slot with a technician, and is technician time or the room internally charged?
3. Which student-borrowable fixture gives the narrowest controllable beam, and which RGB fixtures/DMX interface can be approved together?
4. Which projector is bright enough for the chosen throw distance and green scenic object, and what lens/cable/screen comes with it?
5. May the room be fully blacked out? What emergency lighting must remain visible?
6. Is haze permitted in that exact room? Who isolates/resets alarms, operates the machine and supplies approved fluid? What does it cost?
7. What are the maximum playback level and quiet-hour restrictions?
8. Can equipment be mounted only on floor stands, or may trained staff use the installed grid/skytrack? Who signs off safety bonds?
9. Does the Quest/Tracker project permit cover enough headsets, base stations, straps/mounts and a suitable PC, or are these separate reservations?
10. Can MAGICS or Aalto Studios offer a 30-minute technical consultation before booking high-end mocap?
11. Does Fablab/Set Workshop charge the project for filament, sheet goods, paint or fasteners, and may a scenic object leave the workshop?
12. Which AYY/association rehearsal space can tolerate projection darkness, directional lighting, taped cables and moderate amplified sound?
13. Who owns the personal data produced if actor video, voice, skeleton or headset telemetry is recorded, and when will raw data be deleted?

## Bottom line

The first move is not broad “resource research” or collecting impressive devices. It is to turn the artistic intent into a **small institutional request that Aalto staff can approve**: one room, two hours, five cues, one actor, one operator, a fallback, and an exact equipment bundle. Experimental Studio 1 is the best technical target; Design Factory Stage is the pragmatic fallback; Teekkarispeksi + DADA/DOT are the fastest collaborator bridge. XR, 360 projection and motion capture are valuable comparison conditions only after the colour–light–projection–sound grammar works as one repeatable scene.
