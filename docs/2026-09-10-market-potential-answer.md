# Market potential — research and application draft

Research checked 10 September 2026. Proposed customers and advantages are hypotheses, not validated demand or partnerships.

## Application answer

My long-term ambition is to build an original immersive entertainment brand, inspired by Disney’s ability to turn imagined worlds into places people want to visit. My initial customers would be adults already spending on immersive theatre, cultural attractions and distinctive experiences, including couples, groups of friends and cultural tourists. Over time, different productions could reach families and wider audiences. Theatres and cultural destinations could also become customers by commissioning or licensing productions.

The underlying demand is for wonder, discovery and meaningful connection. I believe shared physical experiences can become especially valuable as more of everyday life is mediated by AI and screens. Audiences may not ask for this particular format before experiencing it, but they already seek memorable ways to spend time together. My aim is to give those desires a new artistic form through stories about dreams and memory that require no prior knowledge of a particular book or franchise.

Depending on location, competitors would include immersive productions such as Sleep No More and Only Dream of the Red Chamber, alongside immersive exhibitions and themed attractions competing for visitors’ time and leisure budgets. Some venues could also become potential hosting partners.

My intended distinction is how audience participation shapes the live encounter. I want to combine AI-responsive environments, real-time 3D visuals, spatial sound and physical stagecraft, such as revolving stages and movable scenery, with live performers. A visitor’s words or actions could change the surrounding world, prompting an actor’s response and becoming part of the unfolding experience. AI would help create unexpected moments of human connection within a coherent artistic world.

Commercial potential would come from ticketed experiences and, eventually, repeatable productions that can be adapted for different venues. The proposition to test is whether audiences find this combination compelling enough to pay for, recommend and revisit.

## Research corrections

- Disney comparison concerns its parks/experiences business, not the whole conglomerate. Official offerings explicitly address families, adults without children and repeat passholders, including residents. These are overlapping segments, not a measured demographic breakdown. Adult purchasers/planners and child/adult participants should be distinguished. The inference for this project is that buyers purchase leisure, shared memories and discovery; it is not evidence all Disney customers will buy this production.
- Sleep No More director Felix Barrett explicitly says audiences can find a story without knowing Macbeth. Its intended audience is curious and willing to explore independently; no representative demographic data was established here. Dreams and memory are a creative positioning choice, not proof of universal appeal.
- UCLA Xanadu already used images, 3D objects, poetry and choreography in extended reality, combining Unreal Engine 5 and physical scenery. It cannot fairly be dismissed as only image generation. This research does not establish that it is the only AI theatre project.
- Only Dream of the Red Chamber is a large immersive theatre/cultural tourism destination. Its June 2026 expansion includes audience interaction. The user's view of intimacy being underused remains a personal response to their visit. No public route confirming external show residency was found; possible hosting is speculative.
- Hamilton uses independently moving concentric double revolves (double turntables). Rewind describes an effect, not the equipment. Rendering a 3D object does not make a physical object: combine projection/real-time visuals with actual scenery and stage machinery. These are proposed creative methods, not yet demonstrated capabilities of this project.
- Demand creation: the specific format may be unfamiliar, while desires for play, connection and memorable leisure already exist. Quality alone does not establish willingness to pay, reach, repeat demand or feasible operating costs.
